# Type: free, premium, and how to choose

Written 2026-11-22. Free sources and the specimen method stay in `disojo-site-system/references/free-type.md`. This file adds paid foundry fonts and the positions behind choosing.

## Position: the flagship usually deserves a paid face
- What the market sells at the floor uses free fonts: the Trussly deck in the Softriver reel is set in Manrope. The 84 sites we measured that look expensive almost all use foundry fonts (Neue Haas, Suisse, GT America, Aeonik, ABC Diatype, Ogg, Editorial Old, Tobias, Neue Montreal).
- A font carries the brand on every word of every page. For a $12k to $20k flagship, a licence is a small line in the budget and one of the clearest differences between us and a template.
- Free is still right when a free face is genuinely the best fit (Level's Nippo, Postcards' Bonny, both Fontshare, both loved). Never paid for the sake of paid.
- For $4,000 Branding: free by default, paid if the client agrees to buy the licence.

## Licensing (get it right, it is the client's legal risk)
- The licence is bought **in the client's name**, for their use: desktop (their team makes documents), web (the site), app (if any), and sometimes logo or broadcast. Each foundry splits these differently; read the EULA each time.
- Logo use: outlining a font into a wordmark is allowed by most desktop licences, but not all. Check before the mark is built on it.
- Who pays: `___` (Tejjo to decide: included in the fee, or passed through at cost). Never promise a client either way until decided.
- Exploration: many independent foundries offer trial fonts for pitching and testing (examples to check, not verified on this machine: Klim, Grilli Type, Dinamo, Displaay, Colophon, Production Type, Pangram Pangram, Fontwerk, Indian Type Foundry). Trials are for the specimen and the presentation only; nothing ships until the licence is bought. Never use a cracked or "free download" copy of a paid font.
- Record family, foundry, licence type, buyer and date in `brand/tokens.json` and the guidelines book.

## How to choose (the senior part)
1. **Start from the idea and the mark, not from a mood.** The type has to sit beside the mark without competing or going limp. Level: a soft ink blot needed something heavy and smooth (Nippo). Postcards: a block-print arch needed weight and warmth (Bonny 700).
2. **Weight first.** Tejjo's two type verdicts both asked for more weight ("thicker, smoother", "something thick with more weight"). Show heavy faces in the first round unless the idea demands light.
3. **One trait you can name.** Each candidate needs a reason in five words: "squared and soft", "sharp ink traps", "wide and calm". If I cannot name it, it is a default grotesk and goes.
4. **One family is usually enough.** A display and a text weight of the same family, or one display face and one quiet text face. Three families is almost always a mistake.
5. **Set it in their words, at real sizes**: the headline, a paragraph, a price, a nav row, a label at 11 px, on their colour. Never "The quick brown fox".
6. **India and bilingual:** if the client speaks to Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or other readers, the face needs a matching script, or a partner designed for it (Indian Type Foundry and Ek Type cover this; check). Decide at strategy, not after the site is built.

## The specimen
15 to 20 candidates, free and paid mixed, each set the same way in the client's words, on one sheet, numbered. My pick and why on top. Tejjo picks, then the client sees two or three in context, not twenty.

## What fails
- The safe Google grotesk (Inter, Manrope, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta) on a flagship. It says template.
- Twenty fonts that all look alike (Level type round 1: "redo, all look the same").
- Thin and medium weights next to a strong mark (Postcards round 1).
- A script or handwriting face added for warmth. Tejjo called that "usual ai slop" on Postcards.
