# Marks: wordmark, symbol, monogram, or both

Written 2026-11-22 as a senior designer's positions, checked against Tejjo's verdicts (`taste-library/identity.md`). Positions, not laws: argue them, and change them only for a reason.

## The question before drawing anything
**Where will this mark live most of the time, and how big?** An app icon at 40 px, a shopfront at 4 m, a bottle label at 3 cm, a LinkedIn avatar, a stamp. The answer picks the type of mark more than taste does.

## The types and when each one earns its place

**Wordmark** (the name, drawn): the default for most of our clients.
- Use when: the name is the asset and nobody knows it yet; the name is short to medium (up to about 8 to 10 letters) or has a shape worth drawing; the business lives mostly on screens and print at readable sizes.
- Why default: a new business has no recognition to spend on a symbol. Every time someone sees the name, they learn it. A symbol alone teaches nothing until millions have seen it.
- Watch: a wordmark is not "a font in a colour". It must be drawn: spacing tuned letter by letter, one structural idea carried through the whole word (Disojo round 6: rigid caps against one liquid O). Tejjo's verdict on a stock font with one tweaked letter: "disgusting". So the rule in `disojo-logo-design` ("a custom detail in one letter beats an icon") is only half right: the detail must come from an idea that runs through the word, not a gimmick on one letter.

**Symbol** (a mark with no letters): earns its place only when something forces it.
- Use when: the mark must live small and square (app icon, avatar, favicon, stamp, product embossing, a pattern); the name is long or hard to draw; the business will repeat the mark enough to make it known (an app, a product line, a chain).
- The bar is high: it must be ownable (lookalike check against category and big apps), work in one colour, and survive at 16 px. Level's ink blot and Postcards' block-print arch passed because they came from real material and craft, not meanings.
- Never: a literal picture of the business (keystone, knot, growth bars, a drum, a bottle). Tejjo called all of those clip-art.

**Monogram or lettermark** (initials): a fallback, rarely the answer.
- Use when: the full name is long and the initials are how people already say it (KDC), or the business needs a seal-like small mark beside a wordmark.
- Watch: two or three letters glued together is the most common AI logo there is. Level's geometric L-E sheet: "I'm done with these geometric marks". A monogram needs a real object or construction behind it, or it is a font.

**Combination** (symbol plus wordmark): the right answer for most flagship clients, used as a system, not a lockup.
- The wordmark carries the name everywhere it fits; the symbol carries the small square places. They are designed together, from one idea, so either alone is clearly the same brand.
- The lockup (both side by side) is used less than people think: on the website header and the business card, often neither.

**Emblem or badge** (name inside a shape): only for trades that genuinely use seals (food, drink, crafts, institutions like CTESCOA). Fails small, so it always needs a simpler partner mark.

**A dynamic or system identity** (the mark changes: a frame, a crop, a grid that holds content): for brands whose content is the product (studios, media, events). Print Elements' crop marks are the start of one. Powerful, but only when the client will keep feeding it content; otherwise it looks unfinished.

## Decision in three questions
1. Is the name unknown and drawable? Lead with a wordmark.
2. Will it live small and square most of the time? Add a symbol, or let one letter of the wordmark become the symbol.
3. Does the business produce content that could carry a frame? Consider a system around the mark.

## My default recommendation per client type
| Client | Lead with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Service, studio, practice (CSK, Ascender) | wordmark, plus a small device | they sell a name and trust |
| Product brand with packaging (Postcards, VÆL) | wordmark plus a symbol for seals, caps, labels | small surfaces need a symbol |
| App or software (Level, Mentable) | symbol first, wordmark second | the icon is seen a hundred times a day |
| Local business with a sign (KDC, a café) | wordmark that reads from across the road | distance and speed |
| Institution (CTESCOA) | wordmark plus emblem only if they already have heritage | honesty about what they have |

## How to judge a mark before Tejjo sees it
- One colour, black on white, at 16, 32, 64 and 200 px. If it needs colour or gradient to work, it does not work yet.
- Next to the 3 nearest lookalikes at the same size.
- Can a person describe it in five words after seeing it once? ("a ring of ink", "an arch with a hill")
- Does it carry the feel of the name or a fact of the business, rather than a meaning I explained?
- Would I put it beside the references Tejjo loves? If not, say what is missing, do not send it.

## Constructed symbols (studied 2026-11-23, after two failed Disojo mark rounds)
Every small mark Tejjo rated 5 in the studio study is built the same way:
- Softriver S: two identical heavy leaf shapes, one turned 180°. The white cut between them draws the S.
- Shift S (`andrija/shift-08`): four leaf shapes on a grid.
- Sanora: circles and half circles.
- Cortexa star (`cortexa-02`): four quarter circles.
- Graticule: ellipses on a grid.
- Remono R and the Smash star: the mark is lifted out of the wordmark itself.

**The method:**
- **One primitive, repeated.** A leaf, a quarter circle, a half disc or a rounded square, used 2 to 4 times. Rotate or mirror the copies.
- **Negative space draws the letter.** Most of the shape is solid; one or two clean cuts make the letter.
- **Crisp, calm curves.** Compass-and-ruler arcs. No wobble or hand gesture, and nothing liquid.
- **Rotational symmetry** where it fits.
- **Grid and construction shown** as a book page.
- **Survives 16px** because it is mostly solid and the cuts are wide.
- **The primitive comes from the wordmark** (its stems, corners, bowls), so the two read as one family.

**Who makes it:** code, not the image model. This is exact geometry, which is code's strength. ChatGPT is weakest at it and fills the gap with mush, the D-with-a-moon and the brushstroke O (2026-11-23).
- Explore 30 to 60 constructions in code.
- Cut them to 9 on a sheet beside the reference at the same size.
- Show Tejjo only after that.
- ChatGPT comes in after the pick, for the world: lightbox, badge, app icon on a phone.
