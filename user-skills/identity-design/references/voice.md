# Voice and labels

Written 2026-11-22. Voice is built at the strategy step (`disojo-visual-identity/references/strategy.md`) and handed over in the book. The source-derived method for an existing voice is the `brand-voice` skill.

## Positions
- **Voice comes from how the founder actually talks**, cleaned, not from adjectives. Level's voice ("a sharp desk mate, authoritative, never rude") came from Tejjo's own words and holds because it names a person, not a quality.
- **Three adjectives are not a voice.** "Friendly, professional, innovative" fits every company on earth. A voice is a person, a set of rules, and examples.
- **Write the rules as things you can check**: sentence length, whether we use "we" and "you", numbers as digits or words, how we say no, words we never use, punctuation we never use (no exclamation marks, no em dashes are Disojo's own).
- **Before and after lines prove it.** For every rule, one line from their old copy and the same line in the new voice.
- **Never claim what is not true today.** The voice can be confident only about facts.

## What the book carries for voice
1. The voice in one sentence (a person: "a sharp desk mate").
2. Four to six rules you can check.
3. Words we use, words we never use (theirs, from the interview).
4. Before and after: headline, a product line, an email reply, an Instagram caption, an error message.
5. How the name is written: capitals, spacing, never abbreviated or always.

## Labels: the small words
Every brand has names for its own things: products, plans, sections, buttons, form fields, packaging lines. They are part of the identity and are usually left to chance.
- Name things in the client's world, not in web language: Postcards' "your stack" for a cart came from the idea, where "Shopping bag" did not.
- Short and plain beats clever. A label you have to explain fails (CSK: "What does LM versus MA even mean?").
- One name per thing, everywhere: site, portal, invoices, packaging.
- List them in the book: the product and section names, and the button and status words.

## Packaging and product labels (for product clients)
- A label is the identity at its smallest and most handled: the mark, the product name, the variant, the legal lines.
- Hierarchy in one glance: brand, product, variant, then everything the law requires (for India, for example, the net quantity, MRP, manufacturer and FSSAI or other licence where it applies: confirm the exact list with the client, never from memory).
- Print-ready means dielines from the printer, bleed, CMYK, real sizes, and a printed proof before approval.
