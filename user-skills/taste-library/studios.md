# Studios: what the flagship branding market actually ships

Written 2026-11-22 from what Tejjo sent on Instagram. I looked at every image in the folders below, plus frames from 12 reels. My read throughout; **Tejjo's verdicts go in the log at the bottom.**

Everything is in `studios/`: `nonstop/`, `andrija/`, `softriver/` (full-size jpgs with `*-caption.txt`), `reels/`, and contact sheets in `_sheets/` (look at the sheets first). Earlier Nonstop teardown: `~/Desktop/DISOJO SOLUTIONS/content-hub/brands/disojo/research/nonstopbranding-teardown-2026-11-18.md`.

## The three studios in one line each
- **Nonstop** (Riverflow, TVG, Remono, Smash, Bloom, the DIVE process, testimonials) is **the bar**. Every project has an idea in words, a device that comes from that idea, the client's real product designed inside the brand, written campaign lines, and founder video proof.
- **Andrija** (Graticule, Perimeter, Shift, Meridian, Kernal, Rooted, Cortexa, PaceRace, Footvaerk, Strand, Orvexa, Sanora) is **a good floor at volume**. It uses one dark mockup kit for every client, with a geometric symbol and a neutral wordmark. About three projects in twelve rise above that (Kernal, Rooted, Perimeter).
- **Softriver** (OndaOne, Fancy, Trussly, Tied, Ascend, plus the education carousels) is **the template**. The guidelines deck has the same twelve pages in the same order for every client, and it is presented in the same room on the same iMac. The reel format is the product, not the brand.

What makes Nonstop flagship, in order of weight:
1. **The idea is a sentence before it is a shape.** Each caption tells the story: who they were, what was wrong, the idea, the result. The posts are the strategy made visible.
2. **One device carries the whole system.** Take the logo off a Riverflow page and the dashed line still says Riverflow. TVG's badge set is a merch line on its own.
3. **They design the client's product inside the brand.** Riverflow's app screens and prompt cards, Remono's app and receipts, TVG's site with the founders' faces, Smash's tin and box. Logos on caps come last.
4. **Written lines with a point of view.** "Tune AI to your brand", "Less stress, more control", "Turn your e-commerce into a growth machine". Every application carries a real line, not lorem or a slogan about "moving forward".
5. **Type in a tense pair, from paid foundries.** Condensed sans against serif (Riverflow), display serif against sans (Remono).
6. **Proof is the founder on camera.** The testimonials post alternates the brand cover with a selfie video of the founder, footed "Name · Nonstop × Client · Role". The process is named (DIVE: Discovery, Inner World, Visual World, Execution), photographed in a real workshop, and sold with "Comment DIVE".

Where even Nonstop is weak (what we beat):
- **Their house style leaks between clients.** Heavy condensed caps headlines, checker and badge sets, and the same poster layouts turn up on TVG, Riverflow and Smash. Our rule is a different look for every client.
- **Secondary palettes with no job.** Riverflow's blue, purple and peach, and Remono's lime, yellow, tan and sky, are there so the palette page looks full.
- **Stock outdoor mockups** (the brick-building billboard, the bus shelter) sit next to the real work and cheapen it.
- **AI campaign photography** that could belong to any beauty brand (Riverflow 13 to 16).
- **Outcome claims we cannot check** (Smash "nearly 400K followers", "TikTok Shop bestseller"). Fine for them, but we only publish verified figures.

## Andrija, taken apart
**The kit:** a beanie with headphones seen from behind (Graticule, Perimeter, Strand), a lanyard badge (Shift, Cortexa, Kernal, Perimeter), a lightbox in a dark room, a billboard in a concrete car park (Graticule, Perimeter, Shift), business cards, a tote, a phone in hand, a cap. **The same photos with a different logo pasted on.** This is what Tejjo meant by "not flagship".

The marks are almost all a construction-grid symbol (circle and square geometry, with the grid shown as proof of craft) next to a neutral grotesk or serif set straight. Cortexa is the AI sparkle that every AI company already uses. Orvexa is concentric rings. They are clean and interchangeable.

The captions follow one formula: the problem in a line, "X flips that", three short sentences, then a three-word tagline ("Detect. Connect. Respond.", "One thread. Every channel."). Each client is split across 3 or 4 grid posts with the same caption.

The three that rise, and why:
- **Kernal** `andrija/kernal-*`: the mark tiles into a pattern, and the pattern becomes the whole brand (poster, phone, banner, badge, colourways). The device does the work, not the mockup.
- **Rooted** `andrija/rooted-*`: the O's of a heavy wordmark hold the variant icons (a dashed ring, a star, a moon) for day, energy and night. The product system lives inside the letterforms. This is the best mark in the set.
- **Perimeter** `andrija/perimeter-*`: a serif headline that is the positioning ("The boundary AI can't cross unnoticed"), chrome liquid imagery, and the wordmark cropped huge across a tote.

Also useful: **Meridian**'s voice lines ("Direction over decoration", "We build brands that don't need to shout to be heard") on uncommon colour pairs. **Footvaerk**'s FvA monogram, where the small v gives the only drawn detail in the set.

Weak and floor: Shift (a green gradient on everything, with "Clear strategy for brands ready to move forward" that says nothing); Orvexa ("See Things Differently", "Stand Out Naturally": empty); Cortexa (the sparkle); Strand and Graticule (the kit).

## Softriver, taken apart
- The guidelines reel (`reels/DfU1Wlcowzt`, `Dfhx-EuIrBu`) is always the same twelve pages: Primary Logo, Logomark, Clearspace, Primary Typeface, Type Hierarchy, Colour, Imagery, Graphic Patterns, Social Banners, Mockups, Billboard, Merch. It is the same presenter in the same room with the same iMac, and only the colours change. **This page order is the template floor. Our book must not follow it** (see `identity-design/references/guidelines.md`).
- The work (OndaOne, Fancy, Lycka, Svelvy, Kovet, Ridian, Portly in `DfpfdS3Iyi7`): one geometric symbol plus a grotesk wordmark, set in coloured tiles. Fast, consistent, and you could swap the clients.
- **The education carousels are better than the work.** `c2` "How to fix a boring design" teaches hierarchy, contrast, type, space and one unexpected detail with before and after pairs, and it is good teaching. `c3` business vs brand (the iceberg). `c1` "make your SaaS feel safer to choose". Each ends with "Comment BRAND". This format is worth stealing for Disojo's content, in our own look.

## What we build by (from all three)
1. **The idea is one sentence the client could repeat**, written before any shape (Nonstop's North Star). If we cannot say it, we are not ready to draw.
2. **One device from the idea runs every surface.** Test: cover the logo. Is it still theirs? (Riverflow line, Kernal pattern, Rooted's O, Smash star.)
3. **A symbol has to earn its place.** It needs a job beyond sitting next to the name: it tiles, it becomes the app icon, it animates, it is a mascot with moods. A construction-grid symbol next to a neutral grotesk is the floor. When the name is short and strong, draw the wordmark and put the idea inside a letter (Rooted, Smash, Remono's R). It must be the idea, not a font tweak (Tejjo's rule from 2026-10-21 still stands).
4. **Type is a tense pair**, and on the flagship at least one is a paid display face licensed to the client.
5. **One lead colour with a tint ladder, plus paper and ink.** A secondary only gets in with a written job.
6. **Every application carries a real line** from the strategy, written in their voice. No "move forward", "see things differently", "stand out".
7. **Design their product inside the brand**: the site (ours to build), the app screen, the box, the receipt, the email. That is where the $12k to $20k shows, and template studios skip it.
8. **Real things over the kit.** If a mockup could be swapped to another client unchanged (beanie, lanyard, car-park billboard), cut it. Choose things from the client's own world.
9. **Mascot and illustration worlds** are a big lever for consumer brands: a simple line character with wit (Remono, rated 5), never a busy badge set (TVG, rated 2). Drawing them by hand is beyond code. Say so up front and plan image-model rounds with a redraw.
10. **The case-study post tells a story**, in this order: cover on the lead colour, the product in use, the idea line, the system (type, colour, device), the campaign, real things, the founder. The caption gives the problem, the idea and the result (results only once verified).
11. **Proof is the founder on camera, shot and framed to our standard** (my proposal; Tejjo has not judged testimonials), footed "Name · Disojo × Client · Role". Ask every client at handover.
12. **Our own look stays ours for each client.** Nonstop's style leaking between clients is the one thing the best studio here gets wrong.
13. **Finish is the price of entry.** Tejjo rates polish first: calm renders, space, one clean mark (29 of 34 at 5). Nothing busy.

My read (mine, not Tejjo's words):
- Tejjo rates the **finish**: calm, polished, renders that look expensive, one clean mark, lots of space. They mark down **busy**: TVG's badge clutter and Kernal's wall of pattern (both confirmed real).
- Where we differ: I put Shift, Cortexa and Orvexa at floor because the mark and the mockups could be swapped between clients. Tejjo gave them 5 because they look finished. **Both hold, so the bar is both:** Andrija-level polish on every surface, *and* an idea that only this client owns. The polish is the price of entry; the idea is why they pay us $15k instead of $3k.
- Kernal: Tejjo is cooler on it than I am. I still think the pattern-as-system idea is strong, but the execution is loud. Keep the idea, quieter.
- Mascots: Remono 5 and TVG 2. A simple line character with wit lands; a merch badge world does not.
