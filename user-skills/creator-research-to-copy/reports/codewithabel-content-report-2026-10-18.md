## 2. What works (and why)
1. **Frame 1 shows a real thing.** A sticker in a hand, an app on a phone, an Oreo grid. The brain gets the payoff before the explanation.
2. **Series give people a reason to come back.** "Part 3" is a promise kept.
3. **Comparisons and tests** borrow interest from tools people already care about.
4. **Generosity with a keyword** (stack list, plugin pack) turns viewers into comments and DMs.
5. **Career and identity questions** reach far beyond developers.
6. **Day-N without tension is invisible.** "Day 8" means nothing; "Day 12, client wants it live Friday" means something.

## 3. Your unfair advantage
You run a real studio with real clients, and you have built real tools with AI in the last week that most creators only talk about:
- A full AI video-editing pipeline (Whisper, ffmpeg, Remotion) that went from "mid" (v4) to an approved house style (v9).
- Real bugs with great stories: gray HDR footage, captions vanishing early (easing bug), the "twitching" that turned out to be corrupt frames from video seeking, a stabiliser with the wrong sign.
- 30+ skills that run the studio's content and site work.
- Client builds you can show with permission (Postcards 3D rebuild, Ascender flagship).
Nobody in the sample combines "real agency" + "AI-built internal tools" + "honest failure stories". That is the lane.

## 4. Framing rules
- **Show the thing working first. Then the mess.**
- **Every post is either a build, a bug, a test or a take.** Nothing else.
- **Real material only.** If it didn't happen, it isn't content.
- **Disclose AI.** "Built with Claude Code" is a feature, not a confession.
- **Studio context is the trust signal.** "We use this for client work" beats "I made a cool app".
- **Loud look, honest words.** The identity is loud (cobalt/acid, giant DAY number); the copy is calm and specific.

### 7b. Ten scripts
1. **Part 1** (#2): "I'm building my own CapCut with AI. Part 1. I film one clip, give one line of direction, and it cuts the pauses, adds captions, panels and sound. Version 1 looked like this. [v1 clip] It was bad. Here's why, and what changed. Part 2 tomorrow."
2. **Gray HDR** (#3): "My iPhone videos looked gray in every edit. The footage was HDR. My pipeline treated it like normal video. Fix: convert HDR to SDR properly, with a real tone curve. Before. After. Built with Claude Code; I tuned the curve by eye."
3. **Twitching** (#3): "My edits were twitching. I blamed the camera and built a stabiliser. Then I measured frame by frame: two frames at a time were garbage. The renderer was seeking a compressed video badly. Fix: re-encode so every frame stands alone. Zero bad frames."
4. **Wrong sign** (#14): "I wrote a stabiliser. Shake got worse. Then worse again when I flipped it. The measurement was broken, not the stabiliser. Lesson: check your ruler before you check your work."
5. **Skills** (#10): "30 skills run my studio. The 5 I use daily: [real list]. Each one is a recipe Claude follows so I don't re-explain my taste."
6. **Before/after** (#9): "v1 vs v9 of my AI video editor. v1: [clip]. v9: [clip]. Eight versions of feedback: 'mid', 'twitching', 'looks AI', 'too raw'. Then: 'lock it in'."
