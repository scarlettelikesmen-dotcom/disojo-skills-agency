## A1 (Mon) The Wall Model, the whole idea
Hook: "Every trade I take has a wall behind my stop."
"I trade NQ and ES, New York morning. Before I enter, I check four things. Level: where am I trading from. Wall: is my stop protected. Road: is the path to my target open. Trigger: a one-minute inversion with a CISD, and SMT confirming it when it's there. Wall behind the stop, road to the target. And if two walls fight each other, I don't trade at all. That's The Wall Model. Save this video."

## A2 (Tue) Levels
Hook: "Most traders mark the wrong levels."
"I trade from previous day highs and lows. Weekly and daily fair value gaps. And the four-hour, one-hour and fifteen-minute gaps. Then I read the trend. Then time. The nine-thirty open, the ten o'clock open, and in the afternoon the two o'clock open. And the Judas swing, the fake move after the open. Those are levels too. Opening gaps? I don't trade from them. I use them as targets, because price tends to fill them before a real move. Fewer levels. Better trades. Save this video."
CHART: levels marked on one session.

## A3 (Wed) The Wall (stop side)
Hook: "A random five-minute gap is not a setup."
"My stop needs protection. It sits at the high or low of the structure, inside a fifteen-minute or higher gap. For continuations, the gap has to be sponsored. Price taps a one-hour gap, displaces away, and that move creates the three or five minute gap. That's valid. A gap with no sponsor behind it? Invalid. Straight up. Save this video."
CHART: sponsored gap vs random gap.

## A4 (Thu) The re-sweep trap
Hook: "The first sweep is usually a trap."
"Price sweeps a key high and starts dropping. Most people short it. But it just displaced so hard that there's nothing below to target. So it dips a little and builds liquidity. Stacked lows. Then it goes back up and re-sweeps the high. Now it has something to run to. That's why the re-sweep works. I wait for it, then for the CISD. Save this video."
CHART: sweep, dip, re-sweep, CISD, run.
(Note: 'usually' = his 'more often than not'; do not present it as a statistic.)

## A5 (Fri) The Road (target side)
Hook: "I don't target the big move. I target the easy one."
"Low resistance means stacked liquidity. Failure swings. Equal highs. Equal lows. Price can't take out the previous high, five, six, ten times in a row. That liquidity is easy to reach. I target the nearest of it, not the full draw. I aim for about one to one-point-five. If the first target only gives me one to one, I go for the next one and move to break-even at the first. Save this video."
CHART: failure swings stacked, nearest target.

## A6 (Sat) The Trigger
Hook: "CISD in thirty seconds." (alt hook: "I want SMT at the level.")
"Price taps a key level. I drop to the one-minute chart and wait for an inversion. Then a CISD: a close clearly beyond it. The closer the CISD, the better. I want it less than five points away. That keeps the stop tight and the risk clean. And one more thing: SMT. NQ and ES disagreeing at the level. Most of my trades have it. And it tells me when to exit: if ES takes the high, I'm out on NQ. Save this video."
CHART: tap, inversion, CISD, entry.

## A7 (Sun) No trade
Hook: "Two walls. No trade."
"Sometimes price bounces off a bullish one-hour gap and runs straight into a bearish four-hour gap. High resistance versus high resistance. That's not a setup. That's a coin flip. If I see that, or chop, or big news later in the day, I wait. News first, then the afternoon session. Or I don't trade. Skipping is part of the model. Save this video."
