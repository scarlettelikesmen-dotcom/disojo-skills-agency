## How they communicate
1. **The grid is the portfolio.** Tile 1 of every case study is the brand mark alone, centred on that brand's colour. Every client looks different, so the grid reads as range.
2. **The slides show, the caption tells.** Slides have almost no words, only the brand's own lines. The work is shown living in the world: phones in hands, billboards, packaging, laptops, people. Often 3 to 6 images per slide as a bento board.
3. **Every caption has the same shape:** "New work for X", then who the client is in one line, what they wanted or what was wrong, the idea in quotes, the system (type, colour, imagery), "The result?" with one real outcome, then team credits. Plain, "we", short paragraphs.
4. **Proof is the client's face.** Testimonials are selfie videos of the founders, branded per client, and pinned.
5. **A named process** (DIVE) gets its own post and the bio line.
6. **CTA is a comment keyword** ("RIVERFLOW", "DIVE", "process"), which sends a DM with the case study link. Collab posts with the designer double the reach.
7. **Few, big posts.** 9 posts total; each is a full case study.

## What does not carry over to us
- Their tile 1 is a mark they designed. Ours are mostly websites and systems. Showing a client logo first would suggest we designed it. Our tile 1 has to be what we made.
- The Bloom reel shows the risk: copying a known style is noticed and called out in the comments. Same as Tejjo's Monolog rule.
- Their outcomes are in the caption only, and loose ("blown up on TikTok"). Ours are measured numbers, which is stronger; put the number on the cover slide.
