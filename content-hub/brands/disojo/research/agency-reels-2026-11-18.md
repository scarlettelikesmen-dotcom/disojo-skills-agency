## What the outliers have in common
1. **Borrowed audience is behind almost every outlier.** Famous client (OpenAI, Netflix, Stack Overflow), a celebrity (will.i.am), or a collab post with another account (Allan + Chris Do + Will Paterson, Draplin + record label, nonstop + Jack Watson). The collab puts the reel in both audiences' feeds.
2. **A person beats a sizzle.** The designer-creators (a face, a voice, hands) get 5-50x their studio peers. Polished silent sizzles from digital studios sit at 1-6k even at 13-22k followers.
3. **Frame 1 is something physical happening, or a question.** Hands moving cut paper (Allan), "How do you get clients?" as big text (Allan), the brand motion already moving above a face (Jack). Nobody opens on a logo fading in.
4. **Show how it was made.** Koto's best is the mark being drawn with its construction lines; nonstop's best is paper pieces moved by hand; Hey's is their desk and process.
5. **A topical angle multiplies it** (AI in design: Jack, Hey).
6. **Story in the caption, not on screen.** Short on-screen text, word-by-word captions on speech, long caption.
7. **Length is not the limit.** Top reels ran 28-122 s; what matters is that people keep watching and send it.

Platform signals (Mosseri, Jan 2025, via secondary sources): watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach are the top ranking signals; sends matter slightly more for reaching non-followers. Reach peaks for 30-60 s reels in one large study (Socialinsider). Unverified beyond that.
