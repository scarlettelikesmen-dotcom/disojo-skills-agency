2. The answer to "6 months or followers?"

Neither. Open the free community in week 2-3 and let signals open the paid tier. A fixed date wastes the audience if engagement arrives early, and a follower target is the wrong number: followers don't pay, engaged people do. Evan sold with 26.9K followers; Amber with 34.5K; both had communities long before those numbers.

Planning assumption behind the numbers: a launch needs about 30 founding members to feel alive, and roughly 1 in 10 engaged free members becomes a founding member. 300 free members x 10% = 30. Adjust after the first launch with your real numbers.

Probably 2-4 months at 3 posts a day, but the gates decide, not the calendar. If G1 arrives in month 2, you open in month 2.

Before the gates: the waitlist

From month 2, every keyword DM also asks: "Want first access when the paid community opens?" The waitlist count is your strongest G1 signal. At launch, the waitlist gets 48 hours of founding price first.

3. Trading funnel (@abelkapoor -> Psyche Society)

  1. Reels (Wall Model series, stories, replays, as in the trading report).
  2. Profile: bio "NQ & ES futures | The Wall Model | Free lessons below", pinned: the model, the payout proof, origin story.
  3. Keyword DM (from week 2-3): "Comment WALL and I'll send you the Wall Model checklist." The DM sends the checklist and the free Discord link. Tool: ManyChat or similar on Instagram (you set it up and log in; I'll write every message).
  4. Free Discord: the Wall Model lessons (free forever), a #questions channel you answer on camera, one free live replay session a week. Clips from it become reels (Amber's "Free 1-1 Friday" move).
  5. Paid: Psyche Society, $49/month (your decision) on Whop, when the gates open. What it sells is correction, not the model: trade reviews, accountability, risk and psychology coaching, weekly live calls, a journal template. Founding members: your call on a lower first price that rises as reviews come in (Amber raised hers to $97).
  6. Later: the 3-month cohort at $3,000 by application, when the paid community has steady reviews and you have capacity. Same gate logic: open it when people ask for 1:1.
  7. Proof loop: member questions and (with consent) member progress become content. Never income claims: no "students made", no win rate. Every page and post keeps "Education only. Not financial advice."

How to introduce it on camera (copy, one reel when the gate opens): "For months I've given you the whole Wall Model for free. It stays free. What I couldn't do for free is check your trades. That's what Psyche Society is: I review your trades, every week. Doors open [day], founding members first." Then back to normal content: selling stays at 1 post in 10.

4. Coding funnel (@codewithabel -> paid community), the Amber model for software

What to teach (your Amber equivalent): how to build and sell AI-built software to owner-run businesses, shown with Disojo's real work. Working title: "AI Studio" (TO CONFIRM name). Audience: developers and freelancers who want their first paying clients for custom tools and sites, built fast with Claude Code.

  1. Reels: the build-in-public series from the codewithabel report, plus Amber's trust engine adapted: recordings of real work (screen recordings of builds, a discovery call with a client's OK, a proposal walkthrough with numbers removed), small named frameworks (for example "find the leak, build in stages, stay after launch"), honest personal posts.
  2. Two keywords, two audiences (Amber's move): "Comment LEAKS" for business owners (sends the leak check and the Disojo discovery link) and "Comment SKILLS" for builders (sends the free starter pack and the free community link).
  3. Free community (Skool free tier or a free group): starter skills pack (a cleaned public copy, nothing from the private repo), one recorded build, a weekly live build or Q&A. Clips become reels.
  4. Paid community when the gates open: price your call; Amber's reference is $97/month, the trading tier is $49. Inside: the full Disojo playbook (intake, discovery questions, proposal and staging workflow, the skills system, site starters), weekly live builds and reviews of members' client projects, templates. Founding price first, raise as reviews come.
  5. Disojo stays the high-ticket door: business owners who find codewithabel go to the Disojo discovery call. The community never undercuts Disojo's own clients.

Watch-out: teaching other builders creates competitors. Amber accepts this because her clients (roofers) are not her students. Same here: your clients are owners, your students are builders. Keep client names, prices and private data out of the community.
