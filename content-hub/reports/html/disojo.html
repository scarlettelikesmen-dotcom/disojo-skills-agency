2. The key insight

Nearly all automation and "agency" content on Instagram is aimed at people who want to START an agency. Very little speaks to the owner of a drum school, a clinic, a perfume brand or a print shop about their own Tuesday. Disojo's positioning (find where the work leaks, build in visible stages, stay after launch) is exactly the owner-facing message that is missing. The winners that do speak to owners share four traits: first person, one specific business, one simple mechanism, one real result.

3. Framing rules

  • Talk to the owner about their business, in their words ("the spreadsheet only one person understands", "chasing payments", "is 5 pm free?").
  • Show the thing working. Locked edit: panels with real screenshots, cutaways of real client work.
  • One leak per post. Name it, show its cost, show the fix.
  • Proof from the verified list only. Client-reported results labelled.
  • Calm and sovereign. Never promo, never a price, never "please".
  • The founder is the face. "The founder leads every project" is a differentiator: say it on camera.

6b. Ten scripts (founder on camera; locked Disojo edit)

  1. Leak: bookings (#1): "A drum school was running bookings by hand. Every reschedule meant a message, a check, a reply. We built a student and parent app with an admin panel. Now a reschedule takes one tap. The owner got about 10 hours back every week. Where does your week leak?" (before detail TO CONFIRM)
  2. Leak: retyping (#1/#8): "The most expensive tool in a small business is retyping. Same name, same address, typed into three places. One form, one database, done. If you retype anything twice a week, that's your first fix."
  3. Showcase: Postcards (#3): "A perfume site you can almost smell. The bottle turns as you scroll (check the wording against the live site). We rebuilt it in 3D so the product does the selling. Live now."
  4. Proof: Ascender (#13): "Local inquiries up 70%. Same business, new site: faster, clearer, built around how their clients actually decide. Perfect mobile performance score on our own test."
  5. Method: discovery (#12): "Three questions before we build anything. Where do you lose time every week? What only you know how to do? What breaks when you're away? The answers decide what we build. Not a feature list."
  6. Why no day-one quote (#11): "Why we don't quote on day one. Because we don't know where your business leaks yet. First we find it. Then we price the fix. Quotes before discovery are guesses."
  7. Friday notes (#10): "Every Friday our clients see the build, live, on a staging link. No big reveal at the end. If something's wrong, we find out on Friday, not on launch day."
  8. Myth (#9): "You don't need an app. You need one less spreadsheet. Most owners we meet don't need something huge. They need the one thing that stops the Tuesday chaos."
  9. Founder take (#12): "When you get business, that's when we get business. So we build what brings you customers or saves you hours. Nothing to impress other developers."
  10. Stay after launch (#23): "We stay after launch. Software isn't done the day it goes live. It's done when your team uses it without calling us. We stay until then, and after."
